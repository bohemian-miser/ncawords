Dynamic Kernel NCA — Physics Interpolation & Organism Comparison

In our Dynamic Layer-Modulated Kernel NCA architecture, every cell dynamically synthesizes its 5×5 spatial perception filters from a basis modulated by its surrounding state. This demo lets you compare these models side-by-side on a shared 16-channel grid using the interpolation trick: a painted control field $B(x, y)$ smoothly interpolates between Model A and Model B cell-by-cell ($dx = (1-B) dx_A + B dx_B$).

Architecture: The NCA, The Perception Kernel & The MLP

A Neural Cellular Automaton (NCA) is a continuous dynamical system on a 2D lattice. Every pixel holds a 16-channel state vector $x \in \mathbb{R}^{16}$ (channels 0–2: RGB color, channel 3: $\alpha$ opacity, channels 4–15: hidden chemical channels). Crucially, there are no global coordinate inputs ($x, y$); every cell runs the exact same local update rule in parallel.

1. The NCA Per-Cell Update Engine

At every time step, each cell updates its state through three compact stages: Spatial Perception, a Pointwise 2-Layer MLP, and a Stochastic Residual Addition.

The three core operations:

  • Perception ($p \in \mathbb{R}^{48}$): Stacks the cell’s current state (16) with two spatial neighborhood convolutions $K_0 \ast x$ (16) and $K_1 \ast x$ (16).
  • Pointwise MLP ($48 \to 128 \to 16$): Two dense layers with $\text{ReLU}$ non-linearity propose a velocity vector $\Delta x$. Layer 2 is zero-initialized so untrained models begin by doing nothing.
  • Stochastic Residual Step: Each cell flips a coin ($p=0.5$). If heads, it updates: $x \leftarrow x + \Delta x$; if tails, it preserves its state. This async update prevents artificial global clock synchronization.

2. Fixed vs. Dynamic Perception Kernels

Classic NCAs hardcode fixed $3\times3$ Sobel filters. This forces every cell — whether on the outer boundary, deep in the core, or in empty space — to see through the exact same spatial lens.

Our Dynamic Kernel NCA eliminates this bottleneck: each cell inspects its local $3\times3$ surrounding patch to dynamically synthesize a tailored $5\times5$ perception filter from four fundamental basis operators.

3. The Spatial Interpolation Trick

Because all 16-channel models share the same state space, their velocity proposals $\Delta x_A$ and $\Delta x_B$ exist in the same tangent space. Interpolating model weights ($W_{\text{blend}} = (1-\alpha) W_A + \alpha W_B$) destroys the nonlinear attractor dynamics and kills the organism.

Instead, the spatial interpolation trick evaluates both models independently and blends their infinitesimal velocity updates locally: 

Δx(y, x) = (1 - B(y, x)) · ΔxA(y, x) + B(y, x) · ΔxB(y, x)
x(y, x) ← x(y, x) + fire · Δx(y, x)

This produces smooth physical chimeras across the boundary without parameter collapse.

4. Is It a “Mask”? The Alpha Viability Rule

In software code, this operation is commonly implemented as alive_mask = (max_pool2d(alpha, 3) > 0.1), which often creates the impression that an external stencil is being overlaid onto the grid.

It is not an external mask. It is an intrinsic cellular viability rule (apoptosis) operating directly on the cell’s own state vector ($x_3 = \alpha$):

  • The Biological Intuition: In the original Distill 2020 paper by Mordvintsev et al., $\alpha$ represents cell membrane integrity. In multicellular biology, cells that lose adhesion or contact with living tissue undergo programmed cell death (apoptosis) and dissolve into the void ($x \to \vec{0}$).
  • Why the Original Rule is Sound: For embryonic morphogenesis (growing an organism outward from a single fertilized seed), requiring tissue contact ($\max_{3\times3} \alpha > 0.1$) is a natural local rule that prevents dead space from drifting.
  • Why We Added the Toggle in This Demo: While the alpha rule is fine for seed growth, it limits abiogenesis — spontaneous self-organization out of pure noise soup. Unchecking the Alive Mask toggle lets you verify that our noise-trained Dynamic Kernel models maintain quiet backgrounds and sharp borders purely through their learned internal weights, without needing an apoptosis rule!
