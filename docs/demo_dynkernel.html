Dynamic Kernel NCA — Physics Interpolation & Organism Comparison

In our Dynamic Layer-Modulated Kernel NCA architecture, every cell dynamically synthesizes its 5×5 spatial perception filters from a basis modulated by its surrounding state. This demo lets you compare these models side-by-side on a shared 16-channel grid using the interpolation trick: a painted control field $B(x, y)$ smoothly interpolates between Model A and Model B cell-by-cell ($dx = (1-B) dx_A + B dx_B$).

The Architecture of Learned & Dynamic Kernels

Standard Neural Cellular Automata (Mordvintsev et al., Distill 2020) rely on fixed, handcrafted $3\times3$ Sobel filters for perception. While elegant, hardcoding spatial derivatives creates an artificial bottleneck: the network cannot adapt its receptive field, cannot sense long-range curvature, and applies the exact same spatial filter regardless of whether a cell sits on an outer membrane, deep in the interior tissue, or out in empty space.

Our research explores the evolution from hardcoded perception to Trainable Spatial Kernels and ultimately to Dynamic Layer-Modulated Kernel NCA. Below is a detailed breakdown of the architecture, its mathematical foundation, and why it enables spontaneous self-organization from pure noise.

1. From Fixed Sobel to Dynamic Per-Cell Perception

Consider the progression of spatial perception mechanisms in Neural Cellular Automata:

  • Classic Sobel NCA (Fixed 3×3): Perception is $p(x, y) = [x, \text{Sobel}_x \ast x, \text{Sobel}_y \ast x]$. Because the filters are fixed constants, all spatial reasoning is forced into $1\times1$ pointwise convolutions in the subsequent MLP. The model cannot coordinate anisotropic growth directions or long-range boundary curvature.
  • Trainable Kernel NCA (Learned 5×5): Replaces Sobel constants with gradient-trainable $5\times5$ convolutional kernels $K_0, K_1$. Through backpropagation, the system learns specialized concentric rings and diffusion filters. However, the kernels remain spatially invariant — every cell in the universe sees through the exact same spatial lens.
  • Dynamic Layer-Modulated Kernel NCA (Our Architecture): Eliminates spatial invariance. Each cell $(y, x)$ dynamically synthesizes its own effective spatial perception filters $K_{\text{eff}}(y, x)$ at every time step, conditioned on its local surrounding $3\times3$ neighborhood and global network layers.

2. Mathematical Formulation

The dynamic perception layer operates in three stages:

Stage A: Local & Global Gating Logits

To determine which spatial filters a cell needs, a lightweight $3\times3$ convolution inspects the immediate neighborhood of the 16 channels, while global pooled state vectors $\bar{x} \in \mathbb{R}^{16}$ and $\bar{h} \in \mathbb{R}^{128}$ provide whole-organism scale and phase context: 

Lk,m(y, x) = ∑c=0C-1 ∑dy,dx=-11 Wsurround[k·M+m, c, dy+1, dx+1] · x[c, y+dy, x+dx] + bsurround[k·M+m] + (Wx x̄ + Wh h̄)k·M+m

Stage B: Normalized Basis Gating

A temperature-scaled Softmax transforms the logits into a valid probability distribution over the $M=4$ basis filters: 

αk,m(y, x) = exp( Lk,m(y, x) / T ) / ∑m'=0M-1 exp( Lk,m'(y, x) / T )

Stage C: Dynamic Kernel Composition

The cell’s spatial perception filter $K_{\text{eff}, k}(y, x) \in \mathbb{R}^{5 \times 5}$ is synthesized as a linear combination of the basis primitives: 

Keff,k(y, x) = ∑m=0M-1 αk,m(y, x) · Basisk,m

4. Constant $O(1)$ Memory Footprint During Training

A persistent hurdle with dynamic neural networks is memory complexity. A naive spatial hypernetwork predicting a distinct $5\times5$ filter per pixel would generate $5 \times 5 \times 2 = 50$ weights per cell. For a batch of 16 boards over 64 unrolled training steps, storing these intermediate weight tensors causes immediate out-of-memory crashes on modern GPUs ($O(B \cdot T \cdot H \cdot W \cdot K)$).

Our architecture resolves this by exploiting the distributive linearity of convolution: 

( ∑m αm(y, x) · Basism ) * state  ≡  ∑m αm(y, x) · ( Basism * state )

During the backward pass in PyTorch, we convolve the input with the 4 static basis kernels first (depthwise 2D convolution), and then compute the pointwise dot product with the scalar mixture weights $\alpha_m(y, x)$. This maintains a strict $O(1)$ memory footprint with respect to kernel size and step depth.

6. The Spatial Interpolation Trick

Because all models share identical 16-channel and 128-hidden dimensions, their proposed updates $dx_A$ and $dx_B$ exist in the same tangent space. Naively interpolating model parameters ($W_{\text{blend}} = (1-\alpha) W_A + \alpha W_B$) destroys the delicate non-linear attractor dynamics, causing immediate death.

Instead, our spatial update interpolation trick evaluates both models independently and blends their infinitesimal velocity proposals locally: 

dx(x, y) = (1 - B(x, y)) · dxA(x, y) + B(x, y) · dxB(x, y)
x(x, y) ← x(x, y) + fire · dx(x, y)

Because $dx$ represents an instantaneous velocity field, its convex combination forms a well-behaved continuous dynamical system. Along the transition boundary ($B \approx 0.5$), you can observe chimeras: the fire organism adopts the structural body contour of the rocket, or gecko limbs merge smoothly into alien tentacles!

7. Spontaneous Nucleation from Pure Noise

Unlike conventional NCAs that must be primed with an isolated seed pixel, our new models were trained with continuous sample pool resets starting from uniform random noise $\mathcal{U}(0, 1)$. Clicking Fill Pure Noise shuffles all 16 channels into random static.

Watch the system: within 40–80 steps, cells self-coordinate, extinguish high-entropy background noise, and nucleate into stable living forms matching the spatial rule boundaries painted in $B(x, y)$.

8. What is the Alive Mask, and Why is it an Architectural Smell?

In the simulation controls above, you will find an Alive Mask checkbox. In the seminal Distill 2020 paper (Growing Neural Cellular Automata by Mordvintsev et al.), the authors introduced a hardcoded, non-differentiable rule into the simulation loop: 

alive(y, x) = maxΔy, Δx ∈ {-1, 0, 1} α(y + Δy, x + Δx) > 0.1
x(y, x) ← x(y, x) · [ alivepre(y, x) ∧ alivepost(y, x) ]

Any cell whose Moore $3\times3$ neighborhood has no alpha exceeding 0.1 is categorized as “dead” — and its entire 16-channel state is forcibly set to zero.

Why was it originally introduced?

  • Empty-Space Drift: Unconstrained neural networks easily produce minute non-zero output biases ($\Delta x \approx 10^{-3}$). Over hundreds of unrolled recurrent steps in empty space ($x = \vec{0}$), these biases compound into “ghost noise” or spontaneous runaway static.
  • Embryonic Morphogenesis Constraint: Mordvintsev et al. wanted to simulate biological development, where growth proceeds strictly via cell division outward from an initial single-cell zygote. The alive mask guarantees that cells can only activate adjacent to already-living cells.

Why is it an architectural “smell”?

  • Breaks Cellular Automata Purity: In true cellular automata (Conway’s Game of Life, continuous Lenia, reaction-diffusion PDEs), the defining axiom is spatial uniformity: every cell in the universe obeys the exact same physical law. An external, hand-crafted referee clamping channels to zero from outside the neural net violates this foundational axiom.
  • Forbids Abiogenesis / Spontaneous Nucleation: If cells can only be born adjacent to existing live cells, true nucleation out of a primordial soup or random noise is mathematically forbidden. In Conway's Game of Life, three dead cells birth a live cell ($0 \to 1$); an alive mask would kill that newborn cell before it could ever take its first step.
  • An Inductive Crutch: A properly regularized dynamical system should learn in its own weights ($fc_0$ and $fc_1$) that $x = \vec{0}$ is a stable attractor fixed-point ($\Delta x(\vec{0}) = \vec{0}$). Relying on an external mask allows the network to remain sloppy about empty-space dynamics.
